Subculture As Signal

Research Framework

The Insight

Subcultures are where mainstream culture goes to shop for ideas. Every major cultural shift—from streetwear to sustainability, from hip-hop to wellness—started as a subculture before becoming a market. The challenge isn't spotting subcultures. It's understanding which ones carry the signal of where culture is heading, and which are just noise.

The Framework

I look at subcultures through three lenses: authenticity (is this driven by genuine passion or commercial interest?), scalability (does this have the codes to travel beyond its origin community?), and tension (does this push against an existing norm in a way that creates cultural energy?).

The subcultures that score high on all three are the ones worth paying attention to. They're not just trends—they're directional signals. They tell you where the mainstream will be in 18 to 24 months, giving brands a crucial head start.

In Practice

This framework has helped me identify cultural opportunities for brands like Nike (the rise of women's football culture in Europe), Beats (the creator economy on TikTok), and Foot Locker (the intersection of sneakers and social justice). In each case, the subcultural signal was visible years before the mainstream caught up.
