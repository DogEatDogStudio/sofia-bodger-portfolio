Why Brands Lose Cool

Cultural Tension Mapping

The Problem

Every brand wants to be "culturally relevant." Most fail. Not because they lack budget or talent, but because they misunderstand what makes something culturally cool in the first place. Cool isn't a demographic. It's a tension between belonging and rebellion, between mainstream access and subcultural authenticity.

The Framework

Brands lose cool through a predictable lifecycle. First, they discover a cultural space. Then they invest in it authentically. Then they scale it—and in scaling, they dilute the very tension that made the space interesting. The audience that adopted the brand early senses this dilution before anyone else. They move on. The brand is left holding a cultural position that no one believes anymore.

Understanding this lifecycle is the first step to avoiding it. The brands that sustain cultural relevance—Nike, Patagonia, Supreme—are the ones that intentionally create new cultural tensions rather than riding existing ones to exhaustion.

The Application

I've used this framework to help brands identify where they sit on the cultural tension spectrum, and more importantly, when it's time to shift. This isn't about chasing trends. It's about understanding the underlying dynamics of how culture moves, and positioning your brand to move with it—or ideally, ahead of it.
